# Build status against the master directive

## Strategy: the social network you speak

YAPILAPI is the social network you speak. The core unit is the **Yap**, a voice post of up to 60
seconds: hold one button and talk, with no camera, no face and no typing. Every Yap gets a
transcript (searchable, checked by moderation, the text alternative) and reaches listeners in their
own language through automatic translation, with "Listen in …". Voice is about fifty times lighter
on data than video, which matters for the people we build for first (Africa, South Asia, the Middle
East). Photos and reels stay, but voice leads: the big Yap button sits in the middle of the
navigation (Pulse · Wander · Yap · Chats · You; the chats tab is now called Chats, and the
standalone messenger keeps the name Yap), Yaps come first after For you on Pulse, onboarding starts
with a voice hello, and the landing page says "Speak. The world understands." (naming and details:
docs/product/yaps.md). Built so far: Yaps, voice replies and voice intros (docs/product/yaps.md) and Yapilapi Today, a
daily spoken briefing (docs/product/yapilapi-today.md). Next on the same data: Yap Radio
(hands-free listening) and Ask the city.

## The first integrated flow (directive §60)

Sign up → profile → interests → follow → Home → Discover → create post → post appears → like → comment → follow user → message → create group → community: **Built and covered end to end** by `apps/api/test/flow.test.ts`, and verified through the web app's proxy.

  - Videos are remuxed without their tags. The web MP4, HLS, editor renders, recaps, clips, shared-reel videos and live recordings are written without tags too.
  - Voice notes lose their tags.
  - This covers direct and resumable uploads, view-once media, chat attachments, stories, avatars and covers.

  - verification and password reset emails, whose failures are logged and never fail the request;
  - security notices for password changed or reset, two-step verification on or off, new recovery codes, passkey added or removed, and phone added or removed;
  - a note when an account is deleted.

  - With Analytics off, no events are recorded for that person, and the events already recorded are unlinked from them.

  - used or expired one-time data (email links, challenges, OAuth codes, download links, unfinished uploads and their chunks);
  - finished jobs and webhook deliveries;
  - view-once uploads that were never sent;
  - deleted posts, stories, messages, comments, live chat and recaps after 30 days (180 days when moderators removed them), with the files only they used;
  - raw live recordings.

  - A birth date is required at sign-up on the web and phone, and under 13 is refused. 13 to 17 year olds get a private account and ads personalization off.

  - Account deletion removes every size, web MP4, HLS folder, caption file, view-once file, stored and raw live recording, recap video and shared-reel video.
  - Deleting a recap removes its HLS folder.
  - A live's raw recording is removed once it has been stored.

## "Ask me": a question box on profiles (migration 0045, `apps/api/test/ask.test.ts`)

People turn on a question box (web Settings > Account > Question box, or "Turn on" on their own profile; mobile Settings > Account, or the card on their profile) with an optional one-line prompt ("Ask me about film photography"), who can ask (everyone signed in, people they follow, or friends) and whether askers may hide their name from the public. Visitors see an Ask card on the profile with the prompt and a 300-character question (a sheet on the phone), with "Ask without your name shown" when allowed. A question asked without a name is never anonymous to YAPILAPI: `ask_questions.asker_id` is always stored, blocks and limits apply to it, and moderators see who asked (moderation cases carry the asker as the subject). No other response gives it out, to the person asked included: their inbox, notifications (no actor), the Answers tab, the post that shares an answer, reports and their data export all leave it out, which the tests check by searching every response for the asker's id and username. The rules (`apps/api/src/lib/ask.ts`, `checkAsk`): the box must be on; blocks either way refuse, with or without a name; adults can't ask people under 18 they aren't friends with; the audience applies; a private account's box is for its followers; people under 18 never get questions without a name (the setting can't be turned on for them and is ignored if saved); asking someone who isn't a friend needs a confirmed email or phone when `REQUIRE_VERIFICATION` is on. Pace (`ASK_LIMITS`): 20 questions an hour per asker, 5 a day to one person, 200 an hour into one box. Questions go through the same checks as comments: harmful text is refused, flagged text and spam (`assessQuestion`: links from new accounts, the same question sent to a fifth person within an hour) wait for a moderator and reach the person asked (with a notification) only once cleared; limited accounts' questions are held; the owner's hidden words send a question straight to Hidden. The owner's inbox (web `/questions`, mobile Questions screen) has New, Answered and Hidden: answer, hide or show again, delete, report, and block whoever asked. Blocking a named asker is an ordinary block; blocking the asker of a question without a name is a question block (`ask_blocks`, one row per question so two questions can't be linked): they can't ask again and don't see the box or answers, and the owner learns nothing (it isn't in the blocked list and their profile doesn't show as blocked); it can be undone from the same question. Answering (up to 1000 characters, checked like a comment; flagged answers show only to the owner until cleared) tells the asker and adds a card to the profile's new **Answers** tab ("Asked by @name" or "Asked without a name", then the answer); it can also be shared as a post for everyone, followers or friends, which goes through every post check and quotes the question (`posts.question_id`, `Post.question`, shown by the design-system `QuestionQuote` and on the phone). Answers follow the profile's privacy (private accounts: followers only; nobody signed out sees answers from under-18 accounts) and can be reported (`answer`; questions are reported as `question`, by the person asked only). The Answers tab is in `PROFILE_TABS` and shows only while the box is on or has answers; turning the box on adds it to a profile that chose its own tabs. Deleting the account removes the questions someone asked and was asked, with their answers, and their box; the export lists the box, questions asked (with the answer while shown) and questions received (without the asker for those without a name). Not done: a list of the questions you asked; editing an answer (delete and answer again is not possible either: a deleted question is gone). The phone screens are type-checked but not run on a device.

## Drops (2026-09-27, migration 0047, `apps/api/test/drops.test.ts`)

- **What it is**: a seller (18 or older, like any selling) announces a launch of some of their own products (physical or downloads, up to 12) ahead of time: a name, a short description, a cover photo from their own uploads, when it opens (5 minutes to 180 days ahead) and optionally when it closes (15 minutes to 30 days after), with an optional number per product and limit per person. Drafts until published; editable until it opens (`apps/api/src/modules/drops.ts`, `packages/shared/src/drops.ts`, tested).
- **Before it opens**: the drop page (web `/drops/:id`, phone `drop/[id]`), a card on the seller's profile and a "Drops from people you follow" row on Pulse say when it opens in plain words ("Opens Friday at 6:00 PM · in 2 days", no seconds, no pressure) with **Notify me**, a reminder list and never a payment. Only the seller sees how many are waiting. Shared links have an Open Graph preview and share image when the seller's account is public (`GET /v1/public/drops/:id`). Products in a published drop can't be bought anywhere before it opens and stay off sale after it ends (until put in another drop); a cancelled drop gives them back to the shop.
- **Opening**: a job at the start time opens it and tells everyone on the list (notification `drop_opened`, pushed). Products are bought through the usual orders and checkout (`lib/drops.ts`, called from `POST /v1/orders`): each order takes its units in one SQL statement that checks what is left (`drop_items.taken`, with a CHECK so it can never pass the number), and the per-person limit is counted while that row is locked. Tests run 12 parallel orders for 5 units, and 8 parallel transactions with no other lock, and never sell more. Units in an unpaid order are held 15 minutes, then go back on sale; a payment that arrives later is kept if the units are still there and refunded otherwise. Refunds give units back. The drop ends at its end time or when everything has sold (the seller is told), and opens again if a hold ends before its end time.
- **Seller**: waiting count, paid orders, units sold and held, sales by currency and when each product sold out; edit or publish before it opens; cancel a scheduled or open drop (everyone waiting is told; nothing was charged before it opened; unpaid orders are cancelled; paid orders follow the usual refunds). Making and editing drops is on the web; the phone shows the drop, the numbers and Cancel.
- **Buyers**: "Your drops" (web `/drops`, account menu; phone Profile > Your drops): drops you are waiting for, what you bought in drops, and your own launches.
- **Safety**: blocked people (either way) can't see a drop, ask to be reminded or buy from it; a private account's drops are for followers; drafts are the seller's alone. People under 18 can look and ask to be reminded; buying follows the usual commerce rules. Drops can be reported (`drop` report target; removing one hides and stops it).

**Not done in this round:** making or editing a drop on the phone (it links to the web), buying on the phone (web checkout, like the rest of the app), drops in the sitemap, and a moderator's view of a reported drop's cover beyond its seller and link.

## Games in chats (2026-09-27, migration 0046, `apps/api/test/chat-games.test.ts`)

Four up (connect four on a 7 by 6 board, two players), Noughts (noughts and crosses, two players) and Word ladder (change one letter at a time from a start word to a target word, 2 to 6 players), turn by turn, in one-to-one chats and groups. The rules are pure, deterministic functions in `packages/shared/src/games` (tested in `games.test.ts`: wins across, down and on both diagonals, draws, full columns, taken squares, turn order, words not in the list, more than one letter changed, repeats, passes, forfeits and time-outs), so the server checks every move with the same code the apps use to draw the board. Word ladder uses a bundled list of about 2,000 everyday four-letter English words (`games/words.ts`, no downloads); its start and target come from a seed and are always 3 to 5 steps apart; a player can pass, and it's a draw when everyone passes in a row or the ladder reaches 30 rungs.

- Web: "Play a game" in the chat's + menu (which now opens upwards, so it stays on screen on phones) opens a sheet to pick the game and, in groups, who plays. Mobile: the same in the + sheet. The game is a card in the chat with a picture of the board, whose turn it is and "Your turn" / "Open board" / "Watch"; the board opens in a sheet with forfeit, rematch and the quiet tally for that game in the chat ("Wins here: Ada 3, Sam 2"). When a game ends, a line says so ("Ada won Four up", "Noughts ended in a draw").
- Accessibility: on the web the boards are grids you move around with the arrow keys (Four up: a button per column, "Column 4, 2 free spaces, from the bottom: Ada, Sam"; Noughts: a button per square, "Row 2, column 2, empty"), and a polite live region reads out each move and whose turn it is. On the phone every column and square has the same labels, the status is a live region and is also announced on iOS. Discs differ by more than colour (the second player's have a ring); columns and squares are at least 44 points; checked with axe in light and dark, at desktop and 375px widths.
- Rules (`apps/api/src/modules/chat-games.ts`, `lib/chat-games.ts`): only people in the chat can see a game, and only its players can move. Starting one follows the rules for messages: in one-to-one chats blocks either way, minor safety and "who can message you"; in groups, the people chosen must be in the chat and none of them may have blocked another. Up to 6 games go at once in a chat, at most 3 of the same kind (see "Several games at once" below). Every move carries the board's move number (a move for an older board gets 409 `game_moved_on`) and a client move id (a double tap or retry isn't played twice); moves are made under a row lock. Moves, forfeits and endings go to everyone in the chat over the realtime socket (`game.updated`); nothing is pushed to phones. A game with no move for 24 hours ends unfinished (a job queued after each move). Rematch: once it's over, any player can ask; everyone asking gets the same rematch, with the next player going first. Unsending the card removes the game; disappearing chats take it with the card. The data export lists the games you played.

**Not done in this round:** games in community chats; choosing the words for a Word ladder; leaving a group doesn't forfeit your games (they end after a day without a move instead). The phone screens are type-checked but not run on a device.

## Photo collages (2026-09-28, migration 0049, `apps/api/test/collages.test.ts`)

2 to 9 of your photos put together in one picture for a post or a story, drawn on the server with sharp at full quality. The layouts live in `packages/shared/src/collage.ts` as plain data (cells as fractions of the canvas, a turn in degrees for scrapbooks), with the gap, corner, print-edge and crop arithmetic next to them, so the web preview (CSS), the phone preview (Views) and the renderer (`apps/api/src/lib/collage.ts`) place every photo the same way. 23 layouts: side by side, one above the other, one large and two small, one wide and two below, three columns, grids of four, six, eight and nine, one large with three, four or five small, four strips, two above and three below, one wide and six below, and a scrapbook (loose, slightly turned photos with a white print edge) for every number from 2 to 9. Shapes: square 1:1 (2160 by 2160), portrait 4:5 (1728 by 2160) and story 9:16 (1215 by 2160). Gap none, thin or wide; corners sharp, soft or round; ten backgrounds, each with a black or white "ink" that reads on it at 4.5:1 or more. `collage.test.ts` checks that every cell stays inside the canvas in every shape (turned scrapbook prints included) and that grid cells never overlap and fill the canvas.

- Web: in Create, "Make a collage" appears on a post with two or more photos (the first nine go in, and the collage takes the place of those photos); on a story, "Collage from photos" picks 2 to 9 photos and uploads them as they are. The editor (a dialog) waits until each photo has been processed, then shows the live preview: pick a layout (drawn as a small diagram), shape, gap, corners and background; click a photo and then another to swap them; drag a photo to choose the part that shows; zoom buttons for the chosen photo. Mobile: "Make a collage" (posts) and "Collage from photos" (stories) pick 2 to 9 photos, upload them, then open the same editor full screen: tap to choose and swap, drag to move a photo in its frame, pinch to zoom.
- Accessibility: layouts are labelled buttons ("Layout: one large and two small"), cells are buttons ("Cell 2, photo 3. Swap", with a "Chosen" state), backgrounds are named ("Background: sage"); on the web the cells work with the keyboard (Enter chooses and swaps, arrow keys move the photo in its frame, plus and minus zoom, Escape lets go) and swaps are read out in a live region; on the phone each cell has zoom in, zoom out and centre actions and swaps are announced. Buttons and swatches are 44 points.
- API: `POST /v1/media/collage` (30 an hour) takes the layout id, shape, gap, corners, background and each cell's photo, focus point and zoom (checked with zod: the layout's number of cells, each photo once, focus 0 to 1, zoom 1 to 3). Every photo must be the caller's own (anyone else's, or a missing one, is a 404), a photo (not a video or GIF), not blocked by the automated check, and already processed: the media job has made its sizes and, when checks are on, checked it (409 `media_processing` until then; `GET /v1/media/:id` now says `processed`, and the apps wait for it). Photos are up to 50 MB each and 150 MB together, and at most 100 million pixels. The collage is a JPEG with no metadata, stored like an upload and put through the same media job (sizes, blurred preview, automated check); a collage with a photo marked sensitive is sensitive from the start and stays so after its own check (`inheritedVerdict`), so it is blurred for adults and never sent to people under 18. A client key makes it idempotent: the same request again gives back the same collage (200), a different request with that key is refused (409 `idempotency_mismatch`), and two copies sent at once make one. The data export lists your collages; deleting your account removes them with your photos.

**Not done in this round:** choosing which of a post's photos go into the collage (the first nine are used; remove others first); keeping the original photos in the post beside the collage; text or stickers on a collage (the photo editor doesn't open on it). If a photo in a collage is blocked later by a moderator, the collage isn't taken down with it (its own automated check still applies). The phone screens are type-checked but not run on a device.

## Mixes (2026-09-28, migration 0048, `apps/api/test/mixes.test.ts`)

Song lists people make and share, built on the music picker and its licences (`apps/api/src/modules/mixes.ts`, `apps/api/src/lib/mixes.ts`, `packages/shared/src/mixes.ts`).

- **Making one**: a name, an optional description, who can see it (only me, friends, followers, everyone) and up to 100 songs from anywhere the picker offers: in-app sounds and catalogue songs. Songs are added from the music picker (it stays open to add more), each once, at the end; the order changes with up and down buttons (and by dragging on the web), as one full order that is refused with 409 `mix_changed` if someone changed the mix meanwhile. Adding checks each song again from what is stored (no provider call): it must still be offered, playable in the adder's country, and cleared for business use when the mix's owner has a business account; a sound must be one the adder may see or use. The cover is a mosaic of the first four song covers (one cover when there are fewer), and no cover on Data saver (the apps don't load it, and lite API responses leave it out). Web `/mixes` (account menu: Your mixes) and `/mixes/:id`; phone `mixes` and `mixes/[id]` (Profile > Your mixes).
- **Listening**: a mini player plays each song's allowed part one after another: from the start, as long as the licence allows (30 seconds at most, as elsewhere). Each song is checked for the listener's countries and the owner's account type every time the mix is loaded; songs that can't play are greyed with a plain reason ("Not available in your country", "No longer available", "Not available right now", "Not cleared for business accounts", "A sound you can't see", whose name is then left out) and are skipped. Nothing plays by itself and nothing loads before play is pressed. Catalogue songs in mixes are kept current by the music refresh job.
- **In chats**: the owner shares a mix into a one-to-one chat or a group (the mix page's menu, or "Share a mix" in the chat's + menu). The card is a message; while it is in the chat and the owner is still there, everyone in the chat can add and reorder songs and take off the ones they added (the owner can take off any). Each song says who added it. Adding songs writes a line in each chat the mix is shared into that the person is in ("Ada added 3 songs to Road trip"); more adds by the same person within 10 minutes raise the line's count (`message.system` live update) instead of adding lines. Changes go live to the owner and the chat (`mix.updated`); nothing is pushed. Sharing follows the messaging rules (blocks either way, minor safety, who can message whom, group safety, message pace); a mix only its owner sees can't be shared. Unsending the card, leaving the chat, making the mix private or "Stop sharing with" ends it for that chat.
- **Profiles and posts**: a Mixes tab (last in `PROFILE_TABS`, so saved tab orders keep working) shows the mixes the viewer may see and appears only when there is at least one; making a first mix adds it to a profile that chose its tabs. A mix can be shared as a post: its card (`Post.mix`, design-system `MixTile`) shows each viewer the mix only while they may see it, otherwise a note that it isn't available. The owner can post their mix (not an only-me one); others can post public mixes. Mixes can be liked and saved (Your mixes > Saved).
- **Safety**: blocks either way hide a mix, its profile tab, likes, saves and reports; a private account's public mixes are for its followers, and an under-18's public mix is read as followers only. Mixes can be reported (`mix` report target, added to the full list in SQL); a moderator can restrict (owner only) or remove one.
- **Your data**: the export lists your mixes with their songs in order and who added each, the songs you added to other people's mixes, and your likes and saves. Deleting the account deletes your mixes; songs you added to other people's mixes stay, "Added by a former member", and your likes come off the counts.

**Not done in this round:** notifications about mixes (only the chat lines), reordering by dragging on the phone (up and down buttons instead), choosing which part of a song plays in a mix, and mixes in search. The phone screens are type-checked but not run on a device.

## Editing cover photos (2026-09-28, migration 0053, `apps/api/test/cover-edit.test.ts`)

- **One shape**: covers are 8:3 everywhere (`COVER_RATIO` in `packages/shared/src/cover.ts`). The profile header on the web and the phone, the cover sheet and the style previews all show that shape, so the band shows what was framed.
- **Choosing a cover** (web cover sheet, mobile cover menu): upload a new photo, or pick one of your recent photos (`GET /v1/me/cover/photos`: your own finished photos that aren't sensitive, blocked, view-once, animated or an earlier cover copy). Either opens the **cover editor** (web `apps/web/components/editor/CoverEditor.tsx`, mobile `apps/mobile/lib/cover-editor.tsx`), built from the photo editor's parts: the photo moves under a fixed 8:3 band (drag and a zoom slider on the web, drag and pinch on the phone, up to 4×), turn left or right, flip, straighten (up to 45° either way, enlarged just enough to leave no empty corners), the editor's looks with a **strength** slider, the adjustments (brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth, fade, vignette, sharpen), undo and reset, and a **Preview** of the cover behind a copy of the profile header (fade, avatar overlapping it, name, Follow in the profile's accent) in light and dark.
- **Edit cover and Adjust position**: your own profile gets the original photo and the recipe (`profile.coverEdit`, only ever sent to you), so the editor reopens on the original with everything as you left it; Adjust position opens it on the framing. Remove cover works as before. The description (alt text) is edited in the sheet (web) or the editor's Preview tab (phone), with "Suggest a description" when AI descriptions are on.
- **Server** (`PUT /v1/me/cover` with `edit`): the recipe is checked with `coverEditSchema` (built from the photo editor's schema: look, adjustments, turns and flips, plus strength, straighten and a crop; no text or video options), then against the original's real size: the crop must have the cover shape within 2% and not zoom past 4×. ### Chess (2026-09-28, migration 0054, `packages/shared/src/games/chess.test.ts`, `apps/api/test/chat-games.test.ts`)

Chess is the fourth game in chats, built the same way as the others: the server checks every move with the shared rules, the web and the phone draw the board from the same state, and moves carry the move number and a client move id. It uses the same 24-hour time-out, rematch, per-chat tally, live `game.updated` updates and end lines in the chat, and the same limits on games going at once (up to 3 chess games in a chat).

- Rules (`packages/shared/src/games/chess.ts`, pure, no dependencies): full legal move generation, including castling on both sides (never out of, through or into check), en passant (and the case where it would expose the king along the rank), and promotion to a queen, rook, bishop or knight; check, checkmate and stalemate; automatic draws by threefold repetition, the fifty-move rule and insufficient material (kings alone, a single minor piece, or only bishops all on one colour of square). Moves are written in standard notation with `+`, `#`, `=Q`, `O-O` and disambiguation (`Nbd2`, `R1a3`, `Qa1b2`). The state is stored compactly: a FEN-style placement, side to move, castling rights, en passant square, half-move and move counters, a short hash of each position since the last capture or pawn move (repetition counts the en passant square only when a capture there is possible), the move list, the last move and any draw offer. Tests: perft from the start position (20, 400, 8902), "Kiwipete" (48, 2039) and three other standard positions, castling edge cases, en passant including the pin, promotion, every draw rule, fool's mate, a back-rank mate, stalemate, draw offers and the wording helpers (`chess-words.ts`).
- Playing: the person who starts chooses white, black or either at random (white moves first). Moves are `{from, to, promotion?}`. Either player can offer a draw once per move of their own; the other accepts or declines, and an offer lapses after the offerer's next move (`POST /v1/games/:id/draw`, each offer or answer counts toward the move number). Resigning uses the forfeit endpoint and reads "resigned" everywhere. A rematch swaps colours. End lines say how it ended: "Ada won Chess by checkmate.", "by resignation.", "Chess ended in a draw: stalemate."
- Web (`apps/web/components/ChessBoard.tsx`): an 8 by 8 grid of Unicode pieces (with the text presentation selector, so they never turn into emoji) on wood-coloured squares that stay the same in light and dark (pieces 6:1 and 13:1); the board turns round for black. Click, Enter or Space picks a piece up and shows where it can go (dots for moves, rings for captures), and again puts it down; Escape puts it back. The arrow keys move around the grid (one square in the tab order); every square is labelled ("e4, white knight", "e5, empty, move here", "last move", "in check"); a polite live region reads each move ("Ada played knight to f3, check."). A promotion picker, last-move and check highlights, the move list, pieces taken by each player, and a mini board on the chat card. Checked headless with axe (no violations) at desktop in light and at 375px in dark: squares are 44px at 375px wide and nothing scrolls sideways.
- Phone (`apps/mobile/lib/chess-board.tsx`): the same board with tap to move, a promotion panel, the board turned round for black, labels on every square, and squares sized to the screen (44 points on a 375-point phone, smaller only on narrower ones). Type-checked, not run on a device.

**Not done:** dragging pieces (tap or click only); clocks; claiming a draw by hand (repetition and fifty moves end the game by themselves).

## Echoes: answering a reel with your own video (2026-09-28, migration 0056, `apps/api/test/echoes.test.ts`)

An echo is a reel that answers another reel: your video and theirs in one frame, made on the server (`apps/api/src/modules/echoes.ts`, `apps/api/src/lib/echoes.ts`, `packages/shared/src/echoes.ts`; web `/reels/:id/echo` and `/reels/:id/echoes`; phone `app/echo/[id]` and `app/echoes/[id]`). It is separate from duets and remixes, which stay as they were (a duet is still played beside the original by the apps; an echo is one combined video).

- **Making one**: "Echo" in a reel's share sheet (shown when you may echo it) opens the Echo screen. Record with the in-app camera (the camera opens in reel mode and hands the video back to the Echo screen, not to Create) or choose a video. Choose a layout: side by side (720 by 640, theirs first), top and bottom (720 by 1280, theirs on top) or picture in picture (yours full, theirs small in the top corner with a white edge). "Echo after" plays a part of their reel first, 1 to 15 seconds, full frame, then both together. A balance slider sets how loud their sound is against yours (0 only theirs, 100 only yours), and "Leave their sound out" drops it. The preview draws both videos where the server will put them (`echoFrame`, the same numbers on web, phone and server). "Make echo" uploads your video, the server makes the echo, and you watch it (with sound) before "Post echo", with a caption and who can see it.
- **The video** (`echo.render` job): their processed web MP4 (or the upload) runs from the cut's start for the whole echo and holds its last frame if it's shorter; yours plays for its own length; the echo is the cut plus your length, up to 3 minutes. Both are cropped to fill their places (never stretched), 30 frames a second, H.264/AAC with fast start and no metadata (`-map_metadata -1`: no title, location or dates from either file). Their audio (their own, or the sound their reel borrowed from another reel, looped) plays at full level during the cut and at the balance after; yours starts after the cut; the two are mixed with a limiter. A small "Echo of @name" pill is drawn in the top corner with the bundled Inter Bold (sharp, so it doesn't need ffmpeg's drawtext). Their caption tracks are copied to the echo, moved to where their part plays. The result is stored as your media and goes through the normal media job (poster, MP4s, HLS, automated check); it starts as sensitive if your video was.
- **Posting**: `POST /v1/posts` with `format: 'reel'`, the echo's video and `echo: <id>`. An echo's video of someone else's reel goes out only this way: as a plain post or draft, in a story, in a chat, in a Together album, or into the editor or Studio it is refused (403 `echo_not_reusable`, security review 2026-09-28). The video must be the one made for that echo, it can be posted once, not as a draft or scheduled, not for subscribers (now or by a later edit), not in a community, without co-authors, and without another sound or song. The permission is checked again as it's posted. The echo reel has no sound of its own for others to use and can't be duetted or remixed; it shows a tappable "Echo of @name" chip that opens the original. The original shows "N echoes" in its details and share sheet, and a list of the echoes each viewer can see (`GET /v1/posts/:id/echoes`).
- **Who can echo**: every reel has "Who can echo this reel": everyone, people I follow, or nobody. Left unchosen it follows the account: everyone for public accounts, nobody for private and under-18 accounts. It's set when posting (web and phone Create) and changed later in the reel's options (`PUT /v1/posts/:id/echo-settings`); a change applies to new echoes, and echoes already posted stay. Whatever it says: the reel must be visible to you (blocks either way make it look missing); only normal reels shared publicly, with followers or with friends can be echoed (never subscriber-only, sensitive or held ones, and never an echo); an adult and someone under 18 who aren't friends can't echo each other's reels (it reads as "doesn't allow echoes"). The author can echo their own reel. `GET /v1/posts/:id/echo` says whether you can and why not, and `Post.viewer.canEcho` hides the button when you can't. 20 echo videos an hour.
- **Music in the original**: a reel playing a catalogue song keeps the song in its echo only when the song's licence allows derivatives (`allowsDerivatives`: the provider says so, or a Creative Commons licence without "No derivatives"; Jamendo songs do, a partner's only when its API says `derivatives: true`) and the song may play for the echo's author (country, business use). Then the echo reel carries the song and its part, and the apps play it with the echo's own sound instead of muting the video. Otherwise the echo keeps only your audio, and both the Echo screen and the echo say so. Among the dev tones, "Evening tone" allows no derivatives.
- **When the original goes**: an echo is shown only while the reel it answers is there for the viewer too: not deleted or taken down, from an active account, shared with them, and with no block between the viewer and its author or between the two authors (`echoShownSql`, part of `postVisibleSql`, so it holds in feeds, profiles, search and everywhere else). Otherwise only the echo's author sees it, with a plain note and "Keep it private" and "Delete echo". An echo whose original was removed for good keeps `is_echo` and stays hidden.
- **Notifications**: the original's author hears about echoes they can see, batched per reel ("Ada and 3 others echoed your reel", `reel_echo` in the creators category, pushed once). The notification opens the echo, or the list when there are several.
- **Accessibility**: the layouts are radio buttons with a name and a description and a small drawing; the cut and balance are sliders with spoken values ("30 percent theirs, 70 percent yours"); the chip says "Echo of a reel by Ada. Open the original."; status while it's made is read out. Captions of the original are carried into the echo, as above. The web flow (make, watch, post, the chip, the list, the notification) was checked headless with axe (no violations) at desktop in light and at 375px in dark, with no sideways scrolling; the layout radios work with the arrow keys.
- **Your data**: the export lists the echoes you made (the reel each answers, whose it was by username, layout, cut, balance, what was heard of theirs, the video and the reel it became) and your posts' echo fields. Deleting the account deletes your echoes with their videos; echoes other people made of your reels stay with them, hidden from everyone else now that the originals are gone. Echoes are reported like any post.

**Not done in this round:** trimming your own video inside the Echo screen (trim it in Studio or record a shorter one), choosing which corner theirs sits in, their song's volume against yours (it plays at its own level), and an echo of an echo. Their song is not mixed into the file: it plays alongside, as on the original. The phone screens are type-checked but not run on a device.

## Event tickets and check-in at the door (2026-09-28, migration 0059, `apps/api/test/tickets.test.ts`, `packages/shared/src/qr.test.ts`, `packages/shared/src/tickets.test.ts`)

Everyone going to an event carries a ticket in the app, and the host and co-hosts check people in at the door (`apps/api/src/modules/tickets.ts`, `lib/tickets.ts`, `packages/shared/src/tickets.ts`, `qr.ts`, `ticket-schemas.ts`; web `/tickets`, `/events/:id/check-in`, `components/Tickets.tsx`, `CheckIn.tsx`; phone `app/tickets.tsx` from the You tab, `app/check-in/[id].tsx`, `lib/qr.tsx`).

- **Which tickets**: answering "going" gives you an RSVP ticket (the host has none); changing your answer takes it back, and going again gives it back with a new code. While an event has a ticket on sale, answering "going" is recorded but gives no ticket (people get in with the tickets they buy; RSVP tickets given before the sale began keep working). A paid order gives one ticket per ticket bought for an event (issued when the payment is confirmed, once even if the notice arrives twice). A refund marks them refunded wherever they are now; cancelling the event cancels them all.
- **The wallet**: Upcoming and Past (ended, cancelled or refunded; a ticket you gave back by changing your answer isn't listed). Each shows the event, the date and time in the event's own time zone, the place, the ticket type ("Free · RSVP" or the product name), the holder's name, a QR code and a 6-character backup code (no I, O, 0 or 1), always dark on white. Add to calendar downloads an .ics file (built on the device by `ticketIcs`; it never holds the token or code); Get directions opens OpenStreetMap on the web, Apple Maps on iOS or a `geo:` link on Android, with the place's coordinates or its address. It updates live when the ticket is used at the door or given to you.
- **The token**: the QR code holds `YT1.` and 43 base64url characters: the ticket id, a nonce and a 96-bit HMAC-SHA256 made with `TICKET_TOKEN_SECRET` (required, 32+ characters, in production; a fixed dev secret elsewhere). Changed, forged or non-canonical tokens are "not valid". A transfer changes the nonce and the backup code, so the old screenshot stops working; a refund or an RSVP taken back changes the nonce too, and the door says "Refunded" or "Cancelled".
- **Giving a ticket to a friend**: friends only (which also keeps the rule that adults reach under-18s only as friends), not to the host or someone the host blocked, not once it's used or the event is over, and only while the host allows it (on by default; the switch is on the check-in screen and `PATCH /v1/events/:id` `ticketTransfers`). Giving an RSVP ticket gives your place: they're going instead of you. They get a notification ("Ada gave you a ticket") and can see the event even if it's for the host's friends.
- **The door**: the host and the co-hosts they add (up to 10, from their friends; a co-host can step down) see a Check-in screen: live counts ("12 of 40 checked in", updated for every host over realtime `checkin.updated`), a scanner, backup-code entry and a searchable guest list (name, username or code; everyone / not yet / checked in) with Check in and Undo. Results say plainly: Checked in (name and ticket type, with Undo), Already checked in (at what time, by whom), Wrong event (never whose it is), Ticket cancelled, Ticket refunded, Not valid. They're announced to screen readers (an assertive live region on the web, `announceForAccessibility` on the phone). The web scans with the camera through `BarcodeDetector` where the browser has it, and otherwise says to type the code; the phone scans with `expo-camera` (already installed). Nothing needs a camera. Anyone else gets "not found" for every door route.
- **Offline on the web**: when the connection drops, check-ins are kept in the browser (per event, up to 500) with the time they were scanned, and sent in order when it's back. Each has its own id, so one sent twice counts once; one another device got to first comes back as "Check these: Bola was already checked in by Chi at 20:14, before this one was sent".
- **Safety and privacy**: someone under 18 on the guest list shows by first name only (no username or photo) to hosts who aren't their friend, and can only be found by that first name. Failed attempts are limited per person: after 20 that find nothing in 10 minutes the door waits (429 `too_many_attempts`); routes have rate limits too. Every scan, typed code and undo is logged in `ticket_scans` (90 days); tickets and transfers are deleted a year after the event (the privacy page says both).
- **Your data**: the export has `content.tickets` (without tokens or codes), `ticketTransfers`, `eventsCohosted` and `doorCheckIns` (counts per event). Deleting an account deletes its tickets (bought ones stay in the order's payment records) and co-host places, and forgets who scanned; tickets it gave friends stay with them.
- **Routes**: `GET /v1/tickets`, `GET /v1/tickets/:id`, `POST /v1/tickets/:id/transfer`, `GET|POST /v1/events/:id/check-in`, `POST /v1/events/:id/check-in/:ticketId/undo`, `GET /v1/events/:id/guests`, `GET|POST /v1/events/:id/cohosts`, `DELETE /v1/events/:id/cohosts/:userId`. `EventItem` gains `ticketTransfers` and `canCheckIn`.
- **QR on the phone**: `packages/shared/src/qr.ts` is a small encoder (byte mode, versions 1 to 10) that chooses its mask the way the web's `qrcode-generator` does, so both draw the same modules; its tests compare whole symbols with reference outputs from that generator, and its output was checked to decode with a real scanner.

**Not done in this round:** offline queueing on the phone (it checks in online only), a transfers switch in the create and edit event forms (it is on the check-in screen), Apple or Google wallet passes, and printing a guest list. The phone screens are type-checked but not run on a device.

## Market: buying and selling used and local things (2026-09-28, migration 0058, `apps/api/test/market.test.ts`, `packages/shared/src/market.test.ts`)

Person-to-person selling near you, separate from the creator shop (`apps/api/src/modules/market.ts`, `apps/api/src/lib/market.ts`, `packages/shared/src/market.ts` and `market-schemas.ts`; web `/market`; phone Market screen from Wander). Nothing is paid in the app: people meet and pay in person, which keeps it outside card payments, fees and the app stores' rules for digital goods.

## Near you: the live city map (2026-10-08, migration 0087, `apps/api/test/city-map.test.ts`)

What's happening around you right now (docs/product/city-map.md; flag `CITY_MAP`, on by default). Web `/map` and the phone's `map` screen, from Wander.

- **Layers** with toggles: Live now (lives at a place), Today (events at a place on now or before the viewer's midnight), Market (listings by pickup area, on a 2 km grid), Places buzzing (3 or more people posted publicly there in 24 hours), Chains near you (active Pass the Mic chains with reels made there), Friends out (only friends sharing a live location with a chat you're in, or who turned on "Show me on the map to friends"; always rounded to about 1 km; adults and under-18s never see each other unless family-linked).
- **`GET /v1/map`** by box (at most 1 degree each way, 40 items a layer, 60 a minute): candidates per area cached 30 seconds, then filtered for each viewer by the usual visibility rules (blocks both ways, audiences, minors, regional rules). `GET /v1/map/center` starts at the profile's city or a city searched for. Where the viewer is never goes to the server for the map.
- **Show me on the map to friends**: off by default; 1 hour, 4 hours or until midnight; stop any time; one rounded point, deleted when it ends (job worker sweep). In the data export without the place.
- **Map**: OpenStreetMap tiles drawn without a map library (web: images with drag, wheel, buttons and keys; phone: React Native images, drag and buttons), pins that group with a count, a card per pin, and the same results as a list. Set `NEXT_PUBLIC_MAP_TILE_URL` / the phone's `mapTileUrl` for a tile provider in production.
- **Places on posts and lives**: `placeId` on `POST /v1/posts` and `POST /v1/live`; posts show the place (web and phone); "Add a place" in the web composer and Go live form.

**Not done:** "Add a place" on the phone; the phone screen hasn't been run on a device.

## Voice messages everyone understands, and the speech engine (2026-10-08, migration 0089, `apps/api/test/voice-messages.test.ts`, `apps/web/e2e/voice-messages.spec.ts`)

Speak any language, step 2 (docs/product/speech-engine.md). Flags `VOICE_TRANSCRIPTS` and `VOICE_TRANSLATION`, on by default and effective only with providers (`GET /v1/flags`: `voiceTranscripts`, `voiceTranslation`, `voiceListen`).

- **Speech engine**: speech-to-text (`lib/transcription.ts`), translation (`lib/translation.ts`) and new text-to-speech (`lib/speech.ts`: `TTS_PROVIDER` `none` or `openai-compatible`, `POST {TTS_API_URL}/audio/speech`, MP3; no stand-in voice). `speak(text, lang)` returns a clip cached per text, language, voice and model and shared by every listener, within `TTS_DAILY_CHAR_LIMIT` (everyone, per day) and `TTS_PER_HOUR` (per person); clips go when nothing they're for is left (worker sweep).
- **Voice messages**: voice notes and Yaps are transcribed in a job (`voice.transcribe`); members see **Show text** under them (web chat, Yap mode, the phone chat and Yap), translated like a message when they don't understand the language ("Translated from French · See original", kind `transcript`), with **Listen in {language}**. Never view-once or disappearing messages; members only, blocks and hides respected; deleted with the message; your own in the data export. **Transcribe my voice messages** (Settings > AI helpers, and Yap's settings), on by default; off deletes the old ones. A voice note's push says its first words when they're ready within 15 seconds.
- **Production**: Groq recommended for speech-to-text, OpenAI `gpt-4o-mini-tts` or `tts-1` for listening (docs/operations/launch-setup.md, sections 7 and 8; `render.yaml`, `render.free.yaml`; the launch check).

**Not done:** automatic captions for videos without any (still asked for in the video editor), suggested replies from transcripts, transcripts of audio posts through this job (they keep their caption track), and the phone screens haven't been run on a device.
