# Build status against the master directive

Last updated 2026-09-27 (account and chat additions, migration 0041; watch together, weekly wrap, On this day on Pulse, migration 0043; Ask me, chat games and drops, migrations 0045 to 0047). Legend: **Built** = UI + API + database + validation + authorization + tests. **API** = working, tested endpoints without a dedicated UI yet. **Schema** = tables and design exist, no endpoints. **Not started** = nothing yet.

## The first integrated flow (directive §60)

Sign up → profile → interests → follow → Home → Discover → create post → post appears → like → comment → follow user → message → create group → community: **Built and covered end to end** by `apps/api/test/flow.test.ts`, and verified through the web app's proxy.

## "Ask me": a question box on profiles (migration 0045, `apps/api/test/ask.test.ts`)

People turn on a question box (web Settings > Account > Question box, or "Turn on" on their own profile; mobile Settings > Account, or the card on their profile) with an optional one-line prompt ("Ask me about film photography"), who can ask (everyone signed in, people they follow, or friends) and whether askers may hide their name from the public. Visitors see an Ask card on the profile with the prompt and a 300-character question (a sheet on the phone), with "Ask without your name shown" when allowed. A question asked without a name is never anonymous to YAPILAPI: `ask_questions.asker_id` is always stored, blocks and limits apply to it, and moderators see who asked (moderation cases carry the asker as the subject). No other response gives it out, to the person asked included: their inbox, notifications (no actor), the Answers tab, the post that shares an answer, reports and their data export all leave it out, which the tests check by searching every response for the asker's id and username. The rules (`apps/api/src/lib/ask.ts`, `checkAsk`): the box must be on; blocks either way refuse, with or without a name; adults can't ask people under 18 they aren't friends with; the audience applies; a private account's box is for its followers; people under 18 never get questions without a name (the setting can't be turned on for them and is ignored if saved); asking someone who isn't a friend needs a confirmed email or phone when `REQUIRE_VERIFICATION` is on. Pace (`ASK_LIMITS`): 20 questions an hour per asker, 5 a day to one person, 200 an hour into one box. Questions go through the same checks as comments: harmful text is refused, flagged text and spam (`assessQuestion`: links from new accounts, the same question sent to a fifth person within an hour) wait for a moderator and reach the person asked (with a notification) only once cleared; limited accounts' questions are held; the owner's hidden words send a question straight to Hidden. The owner's inbox (web `/questions`, mobile Questions screen) has New, Answered and Hidden: answer, hide or show again, delete, report, and block whoever asked. Blocking a named asker is an ordinary block; blocking the asker of a question without a name is a question block (`ask_blocks`, one row per question so two questions can't be linked): they can't ask again and don't see the box or answers, and the owner learns nothing (it isn't in the blocked list and their profile doesn't show as blocked); it can be undone from the same question. ## Drops (2026-09-27, migration 0047, `apps/api/test/drops.test.ts`)

- **What it is**: a seller (18 or older, like any selling) announces a launch of some of their own products (physical or downloads, up to 12) ahead of time: a name, a short description, a cover photo from their own uploads, when it opens (5 minutes to 180 days ahead) and optionally when it closes (15 minutes to 30 days after), with an optional number per product and limit per person. Drafts until published; editable until it opens (`apps/api/src/modules/drops.ts`, `packages/shared/src/drops.ts`, tested).
- **Before it opens**: the drop page (web `/drops/:id`, phone `drop/[id]`), a card on the seller's profile and a "Drops from people you follow" row on Pulse say when it opens in plain words ("Opens Friday at 6:00 PM · in 2 days", no seconds, no pressure) with **Notify me**, a reminder list and never a payment. Only the seller sees how many are waiting. Shared links have an Open Graph preview and share image when the seller's account is public (`GET /v1/public/drops/:id`). Products in a published drop can't be bought anywhere before it opens and stay off sale after it ends (until put in another drop); a cancelled drop gives them back to the shop.
- **Opening**: a job at the start time opens it and tells everyone on the list (notification `drop_opened`, pushed). Products are bought through the usual orders and checkout (`lib/drops.ts`, called from `POST /v1/orders`): each order takes its units in one SQL statement that checks what is left (`drop_items.taken`, with a CHECK so it can never pass the number), and the per-person limit is counted while that row is locked. Tests run 12 parallel orders for 5 units, and 8 parallel transactions with no other lock, and never sell more. Units in an unpaid order are held 15 minutes, then go back on sale; a payment that arrives later is kept if the units are still there and refunded otherwise. Refunds give units back. The drop ends at its end time or when everything has sold (the seller is told), and opens again if a hold ends before its end time.
- **Seller**: waiting count, paid orders, units sold and held, sales by currency and when each product sold out; edit or publish before it opens; cancel a scheduled or open drop (everyone waiting is told; nothing was charged before it opened; unpaid orders are cancelled; paid orders follow the usual refunds). Making and editing drops is on the web; the phone shows the drop, the numbers and Cancel.
- **Buyers**: "Your drops" (web `/drops`, account menu; phone Profile > Your drops): drops you are waiting for, what you bought in drops, and your own launches.
- **Safety**: blocked people (either way) can't see a drop, ask to be reminded or buy from it; a private account's drops are for followers; drafts are the seller's alone. People under 18 can look and ask to be reminded; buying follows the usual commerce rules. Drops can be reported (`drop` report target; removing one hides and stops it).

**Not done in this round:** making or editing a drop on the phone (it links to the web), buying on the phone (web checkout, like the rest of the app), drops in the sitemap, and a moderator's view of a reported drop's cover beyond its seller and link.

## Games in chats (2026-09-27, migration 0046, `apps/api/test/chat-games.test.ts`)

Four up (connect four on a 7 by 6 board, two players), Noughts (noughts and crosses, two players) and Word ladder (change one letter at a time from a start word to a target word, 2 to 6 players), turn by turn, in one-to-one chats and groups. The rules are pure, deterministic functions in `packages/shared/src/games` (tested in `games.test.ts`: wins across, down and on both diagonals, draws, full columns, taken squares, turn order, words not in the list, more than one letter changed, repeats, passes, forfeits and time-outs), so the server checks every move with the same code the apps use to draw the board. Word ladder uses a bundled list of about 2,000 everyday four-letter English words (`games/words.ts`, no downloads); its start and target come from a seed and are always 3 to 5 steps apart; a player can pass, and it's a draw when everyone passes in a row or the ladder reaches 30 rungs.

- Web: "Play a game" in the chat's + menu (which now opens upwards, so it stays on screen on phones) opens a sheet to pick the game and, in groups, who plays. Mobile: the same in the + sheet. The game is a card in the chat with a picture of the board, whose turn it is and "Your turn" / "Open board" / "Watch"; the board opens in a sheet with forfeit, rematch and the quiet tally for that game in the chat ("Wins here: Ada 3, Sam 2"). When a game ends, a line says so ("Ada won Four up", "Noughts ended in a draw").
- Accessibility: on the web the boards are grids you move around with the arrow keys (Four up: a button per column, "Column 4, 2 free spaces, from the bottom: Ada, Sam"; Noughts: a button per square, "Row 2, column 2, empty"), and a polite live region reads out each move and whose turn it is. On the phone every column and square has the same labels, the status is a live region and is also announced on iOS. Discs differ by more than colour (the second player's have a ring); columns and squares are at least 44 points; checked with axe in light and dark, at desktop and 375px widths.
- Rules (`apps/api/src/modules/chat-games.ts`, `lib/chat-games.ts`): only people in the chat can see a game, and only its players can move. Starting one follows the rules for messages: in one-to-one chats blocks either way, minor safety and "who can message you"; in groups, the people chosen must be in the chat and none of them may have blocked another. One game of each kind at a time per chat (a unique index backs the check). Every move carries the board's move number (a move for an older board gets 409 `game_moved_on`) and a client move id (a double tap or retry isn't played twice); moves are made under a row lock. Moves, forfeits and endings go to everyone in the chat over the realtime socket (`game.updated`); nothing is pushed to phones. A game with no move for 24 hours ends unfinished (a job queued after each move). Rematch: once it's over, any player can ask; everyone asking gets the same rematch, with the next player going first. Unsending the card removes the game; disappearing chats take it with the card. The data export lists the games you played.

**Not done in this round:** games in community chats; choosing the words for a Word ladder; leaving a group doesn't forfeit your games (they end after a day without a move instead). The phone screens are type-checked but not run on a device.

## Photo collages (2026-09-28, migration 0049, `apps/api/test/collages.test.ts`)

2 to 9 of your photos put together in one picture for a post or a story, drawn on the server with sharp at full quality. The layouts live in `packages/shared/src/collage.ts` as plain data (cells as fractions of the canvas, a turn in degrees for scrapbooks), with the gap, corner, print-edge and crop arithmetic next to them, so the web preview (CSS), the phone preview (Views) and the renderer (`apps/api/src/lib/collage.ts`) place every photo the same way. 23 layouts: side by side, one above the other, one large and two small, one wide and two below, three columns, grids of four, six, eight and nine, one large with three, four or five small, four strips, two above and three below, one wide and six below, and a scrapbook (loose, slightly turned photos with a white print edge) for every number from 2 to 9. Shapes: square 1:1 (2160 by 2160), portrait 4:5 (1728 by 2160) and story 9:16 (1215 by 2160). Gap none, thin or wide; corners sharp, soft or round; ten backgrounds, each with a black or white "ink" that reads on it at 4.5:1 or more. `collage.test.ts` checks that every cell stays inside the canvas in every shape (turned scrapbook prints included) and that grid cells never overlap and fill the canvas.

- Web: in Create, "Make a collage" appears on a post with two or more photos (the first nine go in, and the collage takes the place of those photos); on a story, "Collage from photos" picks 2 to 9 photos and uploads them as they are. The editor (a dialog) waits until each photo has been processed, then shows the live preview: pick a layout (drawn as a small diagram), shape, gap, corners and background; click a photo and then another to swap them; drag a photo to choose the part that shows; zoom buttons for the chosen photo. Mobile: "Make a collage" (posts) and "Collage from photos" (stories) pick 2 to 9 photos, upload them, then open the same editor full screen: tap to choose and swap, drag to move a photo in its frame, pinch to zoom.
- Accessibility: layouts are labelled buttons ("Layout: one large and two small"), cells are buttons ("Cell 2, photo 3. Swap", with a "Chosen" state), backgrounds are named ("Background: sage"); on the web the cells work with the keyboard (Enter chooses and swaps, arrow keys move the photo in its frame, plus and minus zoom, Escape lets go) and swaps are read out in a live region; on the phone each cell has zoom in, zoom out and centre actions and swaps are announced. Buttons and swatches are 44 points.
- API: `POST /v1/media/collage` (30 an hour) takes the layout id, shape, gap, corners, background and each cell's photo, focus point and zoom (checked with zod: the layout's number of cells, each photo once, focus 0 to 1, zoom 1 to 3). Every photo must be the caller's own (anyone else's, or a missing one, is a 404), a photo (not a video or GIF), not blocked by the automated check, and already processed: the media job has made its sizes and, when checks are on, checked it (409 `media_processing` until then; `GET /v1/media/:id` now says `processed`, and the apps wait for it). Photos are up to 50 MB each and 150 MB together, and at most 100 million pixels. The collage is a JPEG with no metadata, stored like an upload and put through the same media job (sizes, blurred preview, automated check); a collage with a photo marked sensitive is sensitive from the start and stays so after its own check (`inheritedVerdict`), so it is blurred for adults and never sent to people under 18. A client key makes it idempotent: the same request again gives back the same collage (200), a different request with that key is refused (409 `idempotency_mismatch`), and two copies sent at once make one. The data export lists your collages; deleting your account removes them with your photos.

**Not done in this round:** choosing which of a post's photos go into the collage (the first nine are used; remove others first); keeping the original photos in the post beside the collage; text or stickers on a collage (the photo editor doesn't open on it). If a photo in a collage is blocked later by a moderator, the collage isn't taken down with it (its own automated check still applies). The phone screens are type-checked but not run on a device.
